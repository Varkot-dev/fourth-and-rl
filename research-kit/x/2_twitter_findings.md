- Dates are as X showed them (a few are UTC, so a Sunday-evening post can read one day later).
- Reply and like counts are as displayed on 2026-11-17 and are approximate.
- Game details (score, clock, distance) come from the posts themselves. Where no post stated a detail, it says "not stated in the posts collected". Nothing here was checked against play-by-play; that is a job for the repo.

## 5. Questions people ask that nobody seems to answer well

1. **If I punt late, can they just drain the clock; if I fail, do I get the ball back?** Asked as an assertion by both sides with no numbers (@BillsChatPod, @Dbrave_8, Campbell's own quote). This is exactly the map burning question 1 produces (time left, timeouts, field position).
2. **Does the model know my defense is bad (or theirs is good)?** @CurtisAllenNFL: "Does Ben Johnson's 4th down analytics take into account their defense is terrible?" Baldwin answers informally ("Their defense is Bad Bad Bad"), not with a number.
3. **Was it really 4th-and-1?** @NextGenStats: tracking data "made it closer to a 4th and 2", flipping a "VERY STRONG" go into a toss-up. How often does the hidden distance change the call?
4. **When public models disagree, who is right?** @Shauncore ("4th Down Bot said kick it. ESPN model said go for it."); @RyanPaganetti ("ESPN and nfl4th had big differences in win probability with a made field goal").
5. **How big an edge is big enough to act on?** Shanahan's "51%" line, and Paganetti's "clear consensus amongst respected models by a reasonable margin". Nobody posts an interval; the bootstrap categories (confident, lean, uncertain) answer this directly.
6. **Is a toss-up being graded as a blunder?** @TechHoopsGuy ("picking heads or tails in a coin flip"); @Tyler_Polumbus's no-hindsight poll.
7. **Up 7 or up 14 late at the goal line: kick or go?** Daboll's "10-point lead" (decision 7) and the Bucs' 4th-and-goal up 14 (decision 6). Fans argue from score arithmetic ("only the third point would matter") without a model.
8. **Why punt instead of running the clock and taking a safety?** @HEBalzak, 2026-09-19, https://x.com/HEBalzak/status/2101412876732805528: "Why would you punt there? Run the clock down as much as possible then take the safety" (the game is not named). No public tool models this; it is on the project's "maybe later" list.
9. **Was a 99% collapse a model failure or ordinary?** Posts report 99.1% (Ravens) and a "flat line at 99% Giants" as shocking; nobody says how many such collapses a calibrated model should produce per season. The blown-lead analysis answers this.
10. **If everyone knows you run on first down, why keep doing it?** @BWall, 2025-01-05, https://x.com/BWall/status/1876077948182409537: "Hear me out: 1. ### What nobody in the thread answers

1. **Where is the equilibrium?** Walder says the optimal pass rate is unknown but "higher than where we are now"; Kollmann and Toscano say defenses already adjusted. Neither side posts the marginal value of the next run versus the next pass, which is what the plan's run-or-pass decision model measures.
2. **Is an average-EPA gap evidence at all?** @3firsts's endogeneity point: the averages come from the current mix, so they cannot say what happens at a different mix.
3. **EPA or win probability?** Same reply: "teams care about expected win % more than EPA". Late-lead running (87.4% run share in the last 2:00 when leading by 1 to 8, per `docs/research/endgame_clock.md`) is a clock decision that EPA averages count as inefficiency.
4. **Does the gap survive by down, distance and situation?** Walder's .11 is a league-wide average; Sharp's posts are single-game splits. Nobody posts the gap in neutral situations by down and distance.
